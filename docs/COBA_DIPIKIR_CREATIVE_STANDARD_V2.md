# COBA DIPIKIR — CREATIVE & ANIMATION STANDARD V2
**Status: APPROVED MASTER STANDARD** · Applies to episodes 006 onward and revisions of earlier episodes when explicitly requested.
**Owner:** Coba Dipikir · **Benchmark:** Episode 002 — "Kenapa kita buka kulkas padahal tahu isinya?"
**Tagline:** *Yang biasa, belum tentu sederhana.*

> **North star:** Coba Dipikir is a character-driven animated short about small, familiar human behaviors. It is **not** a presentation, a kinetic infographic, or a narrated slide deck. Every episode should feel like a little story viewers can understand even with the sound off. Episode 002 is the minimum creative quality reference, not a one-off exception.

## 1. Brand identity & tone
- Observant, playful, relatable, gently mischievous, critical-curious; no lecturing, overstated certainty, clickbait deception, or jargon dumping.
- One relatable experience, one surprising explanation, one memorable comic beat, and a payoff worth rewatching.
- Preserve palette: paper cream `#F6F0E5`, ink black `#20251F`, orange emphasis `#E96B3C`, green as secondary accent. Editorial hand-drawn vectors, intentionally slightly imperfect contours.
- Preserve the established font system from `src/brand/tokens.ts` and approved brand logo; do not introduce new arbitrary fonts.
- **Character is the approved 002 master:** `src/projects/002-kulkas/Character.tsx`. Large round head, small body, thick black contour, orange scarf, rounded hands, short legs, responsive eyebrows/eyes/mouth. No generic stickman or silent static mascot.

## 2. What made 002 special
Episode 002 stages a recognizable sequence in one world: walk to kitchen → approach refrigerator → open door → investigate → close it → return five minutes later → awkward revelation. Its humor is **performed**, not printed on screen. The fridge is a real interactive prop with continuity. Acting, camera, comedic pauses and physical consequences carry meaning.

**Replicate these principles — not the exact fridge setting or shots.** New episodes should have their own narrative engine and visual signature.

## 3. Content greenlight before implementation
A proposed topic must answer all of these:
1. **Hook:** What familiar action can the viewer recognize in the first 1–2 seconds?
2. **Character objective:** What is the character trying to do, and what goes wrong?
3. **Action loop:** What visible cause → effect → reaction sequence can play with NO explanation?
4. **Comedy:** Where does anticipation, awkward pause, surprise, repetition or exaggeration happen?
5. **Reveal:** What short, defensible explanation makes the viewer see the behavior differently?
6. **Payoff:** What final action or reversal makes the concept memorable?
7. **Visual signature:** What original 3–8-second animated moment could make a viewer replay the video?

Prefer topics with **physical acting and recurring props**. Reject or rework topics that can only be conveyed through graphs, text panels or definitions. Avoid mistaking more scene counts, elaborate backgrounds or overexplaining for quality.

## 4. Story hierarchy
**Action → emotion → curiosity → explanation → payoff.** The animation leads; VO is supportive.
- Default a compact 40–60-second mobile-first short. Longer is permitted when action, pacing and VO genuinely need it; never fit an oversized script by accelerating to a robotic rate.
- Start with **action/hook**, not explanatory title cards or long introductory branding.
- Keep the same set/prop world across beats unless a purposeful location change adds meaning.
- Scene economy: one conflict, a few escalating attempts, one reveal. 19 distinct cuts are not a target.
- Use a visual gag, not a text-only joke; allow 0.3–1.2s reaction holds and carefully placed silence when needed.
- Final line/punchline should *change the viewer's reading* of the scene, not merely repeat the thesis.
- Scientific nuance: label a hypothesis/explanation as such. Research accuracy before production if the topic is factual. Do not claim universality or unsupported neuroscience.

## 5. Acting, objects, camera and environment
- Animate **intention and reaction**: anticipation, pose change, eye direction, eyebrows, tilt, arm arcs, body slump, walk cycle, scarf drag/follow-through, and response to contact.
- Real physical interaction must be shown: hand touches prop, door swings, object has apparent weight, object changes state, character visibly responds. Avoid a hand merely hovering beside a prop.
- Stage the story in one comprehensible environment with foreground/midground/background where appropriate. Multiple camera views only when motivated: wide establishes place, medium shows activity, close-up shows recognition or comedy, POV illustrates what character notices.
- Parallax speeds differ across layers; avoid repeating one illustration with uniform translations.
- Transitions mostly generated by action (door wipes, passing pole, character motion, prop match cut, zoom into a meaningful object), not ubiquitous fades.
- Every frame has focal hierarchy: character/critical action first, prop second, explanatory type third. No empty lower-half, over-crowded upper-half, clipped map labels, too-small characters, or visual artifacts masked by UI.
- **Hero motion:** at least one memorable physical or visual metaphor driven by the story, not a decorative effect.
- Do not turn every scene into character + text + character + text; use text only when it adds information the action cannot communicate.

## 6. Technical brand, motion and audio contract
- Remotion project in the existing GitHub repo `nabillazachra/coba-dipikir` as a new `src/projects/NNN-topic/` folder; retain `src/brand` and 002 master character.
- Vertical 9:16, 1080×1920, typically 30 fps; responsive safe composition for TikTok/Reels mobile view. Reserve top for platform chrome and lower area for captions/controls. Do not leave giant accidental visual dead zones.
- Type hierarchy and sizes consistent episode-to-episode. Captions are always **verbatim VO**, short phrase chunks usually 3–7 words, max two visual lines when possible, same font across whole episode. Highlight only crucial terms in orange. Caption position stays consistent and UI-safe.
- Conversational Indonesian VO; use natural pauses and distinct inflection with a coherent voice. **No `playbackRate > 1.0` to force VO into the timeline.** Measure generated audio; adjust script or durations. Do not overlap speech.
- SFX should be **motivated by an action**. Vary expressive footsteps, object contact, ambiences, silence, whooshes and comedic stings; avoid monotonous machine-gun cues or competing music.
- Preserve signed-off brand/character reference. New assets are hand-drawn editorial vectors, not photorealistic, off-brand stock, or unapproved character redesigns.

## 7. Production workflow — mandatory quality gates
**Gate A — Concept & storyboard:** one-line hook, conflict, action beats, visual signature, factual note, ending payoff. No code until story works.

**Gate B — 5–8-second acting proof:** animate the hardest/most memorable action using original character and physical interaction; compare side-by-side with Episode 002. *If it feels like a presentation, redesign before scaling.*

**Gate C — Layout & film language:** shot plan; full 9:16 mobile checks at representative frames; visual continuity, size, text placement, background depth, props, scale and expressive acting.

**Gate D — Complete episode:** implement motion, VO, exact subtitles, music/SFX, and timing from real generated audio. Keep scene continuity. Use native Remotion Studio/CMS to review; do not claim quality solely from source code.

**Gate E — Full QA & editorial signoff:** watch entire episode at normal speed and on a phone-sized preview. Review beginning, middle and payoff at 0.5× when needed. Correct blank frames, crop, overlarge captions, clipped labels, repetitive scenery, poor facial legibility, unsupported facts and audio overlap. Use frame/storyboard contact sheet *plus* actual playback. `CI passed` or `build succeeded` is only a technical prerequisite — **not creative approval**.

**Gate F — Publish & learn:** user explicitly approves/publishes. Do not export MP4 unless asked. After publication, compare actual retention at 1s/3s, average watch time, completion, rewatch, shares, comments and saves with Episode 002. Never guarantee virality or assume visual quality alone explains views.

## 8. Creative acceptance checklist (all must pass before calling final)
- [ ] In the first two seconds, something **happens**, not just text.
- [ ] A viewer can follow the conflict with VO muted.
- [ ] Character physically **acts** on at least one world object and responds to the consequence.
- [ ] Story has setup, escalation, reveal and visually satisfying payoff.
- [ ] One memorable 3–8s signature animation is clearly visible.
- [ ] Acting has readable eyes/brows/pose and changes within scenes, not merely one expression per scene.
- [ ] Framing and environment feel like a complete animated short; no arbitrary giant blank zones or cropped labels.
- [ ] Camera movement and parallax serve a story beat; transitions match motion.
- [ ] Same approved character, colors, logo and typography as previous episodes.
- [ ] VO is natural speed, phrase subtitles match word-for-word, safe mobile placement, zero overlaps.
- [ ] Comedy timing/sound are deliberate; no unnecessary narration.
- [ ] Factual explanation qualified and defensible.
- [ ] Full animated playback reviewed by a human; only then mark creative review complete.

## 9. Ways of working across chats
Different chat rooms can specialize (ideas, scripts, character, storyboard, coding, QA, copywriting, distribution, analytics), but they all defer to **this master standard** and the GitHub `AGENTS.md`. If a new request conflicts, ask which rule to deliberately override and record why; otherwise preserve the standard. A chat's brief should always state episode number, production stage, benchmark 002, the single most important deliverable and review status.

### Key operating rules
- Do not rebuild or fork an existing episode without checking GitHub first.
- Never conflate completed code, successful CI and creative approval.
- On a revision, fix the existing project, run targeted QA, then review before merge.
- Do not silently merge a review branch or publish to social media unless requested.
- If reference assets/VO/video are unavailable, say so explicitly and do not claim fidelity without visual verification.
- Prioritize **quality of one coherent 5–8s acting proof** over speed of producing another full minute of mediocre animation.
